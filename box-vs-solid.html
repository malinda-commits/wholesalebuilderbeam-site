Box beams vs. solid beams: what builders should know.

A practical overview for home builders, GCs and remodelers. Sizes are in the sizing guide. Receiving and mounting are in the install guide. How a trade order runs is in the how to order guide. Terms are in the glossary.

A box beam is a three-sided, hollow, reclaimed-look wood beam, open on top for mounting. It gives the look of a solid timber beam without the weight of solid stock. These beams are decorative and non-structural. Solid stock is what that hollow shell is not: the timber look, and the weight the shell is built without.

When a box beam fits

A box beam fits when the job wants the look of a solid timber without the weight of solid stock, and the beam is for appearance.

  • The beam wraps or hangs below framing. The framing above it stays the structure. The beam is the finished look.
  • You are covering an existing beam line or framing with a three-sided open-top decorative shell. The existing structure stays load-bearing.
  • You will set the hollow shell over a wood cleat or blocking and fasten it from the sides or top returns. Three-sided and hollow, it mounts without heavy solid timber.
  • You can name a height and width of 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 inches, with piece lengths up to 24 feet, and order the runs by linear foot. Larger than 12 inches is quoted on request.
  • You already know the stain brand, type and color and want the beams prestained and ready to hang, or your painter or finisher will match the house on site and you want them unfinished.

A box beam does not fit as structural lumber or as a replacement for a load-bearing member. These beams are decorative and non-structural before and after they are hung. This page is not an engineer letter. Follow the job's framing, local code, and the job's details.
