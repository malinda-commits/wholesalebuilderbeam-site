Builder glossary

Box beam terms, defined for builders.

Plain-English definitions of the box beam terms already used on the sizing guide, install guide, bid spec guide, wrap guide, finish guide, delivery guide, takeoff guide, and how to order guide. For home builders, GCs and remodelers.

These beams are decorative and non-structural. This page defines terms the guides already use. It is not a price list or a stamped specification, and pricing is quote-based for the trade.

B terms

Bid spec
A bid spec is the list that goes on a takeoff or bid: height, width, each piece length and quantity, total linear feet, finish, and a delivery address or pickup. It is guidance for bidding and ordering, not a stamped specification or an engineer letter. What to list is in the bid spec guide.
Box beam
A box beam is a three-sided, hollow, reclaimed-look wood beam, open on top for mounting. It gives the look of a solid timber beam without the weight of solid stock. These beams are decorative and non-structural, as the builder FAQ explains.

C terms

Cleat or blocking
A wood cleat or blocking is what the beam fits over. It is fastened to the ceiling or beam line, and the hollow shell hides it once the beam is set and fastened from the sides or top returns. Mounting is in the install guide.
Crated LTL freight
Crated LTL freight means the beams ship in a crate by less-than-truckload freight. Local delivery and pickup are the other ways an order can go out. How an order ships is in the delivery guide.

D terms

Decorative
Decorative means the beam is for appearance. It gives the look of a solid timber without the weight of solid stock, and it wraps or hangs below framing. It is not a load-bearing member, as the builder FAQ explains.

F terms

Finish
The finish on an order is prestained or unfinished. The height, the width, and the finish apply to every length on that order line, and a different finish is its own line. How to choose is in the finish guide.
Freight
Freight on the order form is an estimate based on the delivery ZIP and the size of the order. Shipping stays an estimate until it is confirmed, which happens before you pay. How the estimate works is in the delivery guide.

G terms

Grid or coffered
Grid or coffered means beams run both ways and cross. Count every run in both directions for its full length, and do not subtract where the runs cross. How to count that layout is in the takeoff guide.

H terms

Height
Height is one beam size on the order, chosen from 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 inches, and width is chosen from that same list. Larger than 12 inches is quoted on request and noted on the order. The size list is in the sizing guide.
Hollow
Hollow means the beam is not solid stock. It is a three-sided shell, open on top, which gives the look of a solid timber without the weight of solid stock. Construction is in the sizing guide.

L terms

Lead time
Lead time depends on beam size, quantity and finish. The ship or pickup date is confirmed with the quote, and any deadline goes in the notes box on the order form. How delivery timing is confirmed is in the delivery guide.
Liftgate
A liftgate is for a jobsite with no dock or forklift. Check the liftgate box on the order form, or ask for a liftgate. How delivery works is in the delivery guide.
Linear foot (LF)
A linear foot is one foot along a beam run. Linear feet are the lengths of the runs, added together, and the order form totals them from the piece lengths and quantities. How to figure them is in the takeoff guide.
Local delivery
Local delivery is one way an order gets to the job, along with crated LTL freight and pickup. How far it reaches, and how to choose it on the order, is in the delivery guide.

N terms

Non-structural
Non-structural means these box beams do not carry structural weight. They wrap or hang below framing for appearance and are not load-bearing members. Do not spec them as structural lumber, as the install guide explains.

O terms

Open top
Open on top means the three-sided shell fits over a wood cleat or blocking for mounting. The hollow shell hides the cleat once the beam is set. How it mounts is in the install guide.
Overage
Overage is extra linear feet a builder may add for cuts and fitting. Whether to add extra, and how much, is the builder's call. The takeoff guide does not set an overage percentage.

P terms

Parallel
Parallel means the beams run the same direction, spaced across the ceiling. Count one run for each beam. How to count that layout is in the takeoff guide.
Perimeter
A perimeter is the beam lines around the room: two runs along the length and two runs along the width. If a wall, soffit, or opening breaks a side, count each piece of that side. How to count that layout is in the takeoff guide.
Pickup
Pickup means the order is collected instead of shipped or delivered. It is chosen on the order with the delivery details. How pickup works is in the delivery guide.
Piece length
A piece length is one beam on the order, and each piece can be up to 24 feet. A run longer than 24 feet is more than one piece, while the linear feet stay the length of the run. How to split a run is in the takeoff guide.
Prestained
Prestained means the beams are finished to the stain brand, type and color on the order and arrive finished and ready to hang. Choose it when you already know that stain. How to enter it is in the finish guide.
Purlin
A purlin, on this takeoff, is its own run. Count each purlin for its length, and count the ridge as one run. This is a layout count, not a structural member, and the takeoff guide shows how.

R terms

Reclaimed-look
Reclaimed-look is the face of these beams. Unfinished beams ship as raw reclaimed-look texture, and prestained beams are finished to the stain you name. A photo of the face is not a color chip, and how to choose is in the finish guide.
Ridge
The ridge, on this takeoff, is one run. Count it, then count each purlin as its own run. This is a layout count, not a structural claim, and the takeoff guide shows how.
Run
A run is one beam line, end to end, along the finished ceiling. Linear feet are the lengths of the runs, added together, and a split line is counted as the pieces you will order. How to count runs is in the takeoff guide.

S terms

Stain brand, type, and color
A prestained order names the stain brand, the stain type (for example oil-based, water-based, gel, semi-transparent or solid) and the color name or number. The beams are finished to that spec. How to enter it is in the finish guide.

T terms

Takeoff
A takeoff is the count of runs and linear feet from plans or a site measure, written as height, width, piece lengths and quantities. It is the list the bid uses once those lengths are known. How to figure the linear feet is in the takeoff guide.
Three-sided
Three-sided means the beam is a hollow shell with an open top, built for mounting. Every beam on this site is a three-sided, hollow, reclaimed-look box beam. Construction is in the sizing guide.
Top return
Top returns are one place the beam is fastened from, along with the sides, after it is set over the cleat or blocking. The install guide does not set fastener size or spacing. Follow the job's framing, local code and the job's details, as the install guide says.
Trade only
Trade only means Wholesale Builder Beam sells to home builders, general contractors and remodelers. Pricing is per linear foot by size and finish and is quote-based. There is no public price list, as the bid spec guide explains.
Trade quote
A trade quote is confirmed after the order and before you pay. Pricing is per linear foot by size and finish and is quote-based, and the order form shows an estimate. There is no public price list, as the bid spec guide explains.

U terms

Unfinished
Unfinished means the beams ship raw, as reclaimed-look texture, ready for your painter or finisher to match the house on site. They stay unfinished until that match is done. How to choose is in the finish guide.

W terms

Width
Width is one beam size on the order, chosen from 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 inches, separately from height. Larger than 12 inches is quoted on request and noted on the order. The size list is in the sizing guide.
Wrap
Wrapping means covering an existing beam line or framing with a three-sided open-top decorative shell for appearance. The existing structure stays load-bearing, and these beams are not a replacement for it. How to plan a wrap is in the wrap guide.

Glossary

Box beam terms, answered.

What is a box beam?

A box beam is a three-sided, hollow, reclaimed-look wood beam, open on top for mounting. It gives the look of a solid timber beam without the weight of solid stock. These beams are decorative and non-structural.

Do these box beams carry a load?

No. These box beams are decorative and non-structural. They wrap or hang below framing for appearance and are not load-bearing members.

What do height, width, and linear feet mean on an order?

Height and width are each 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12 inches, and larger than 12 inches is quoted on request. Linear feet are the lengths of the runs, added together. Each piece can be up to 24 feet.

What is the difference between prestained and unfinished?

Choose prestained when you already know the stain brand, type and color and want beams finished and ready to hang. Choose unfinished when your painter or finisher will match the house on site.

Is this glossary a price list or a stamped specification?

No. Pricing is quote-based for the trade, per linear foot by size and finish, and there is no public price list on this page. This page defines terms the guides already use and is not a stamped specification.
